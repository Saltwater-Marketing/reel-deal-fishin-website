**There's nothing like watching a kid land a shark nearly as big as they are.** In June 2026, several young anglers caught their first shark just off New Smyrna Beach, on a mix of blacktips, Atlantic sharpnose and a few bigger sharks ([June report](/fishing-reports/new-smyrna-beach-fishing-report-june-2026)).

Shark fishing here doesn't mean a long run offshore. Warm water, steady tides and bait moving along the beach bring sharks right up to the beach breaks, sandbars and nearshore drop offs a few miles outside the inlet.

## How a Shark Trip Works

We use heavy tackle, sturdy leaders and fresh bait placed where sharks are feeding. Then you wait for the drag to scream. Most strikes come suddenly: one second the rod is steady, the next it's bent and peeling line.

You fight the fish. Capt. Chad handles every leader, photo and release. All sharks are catch and release.

## When to Go

Spring through fall is the window, and summer is peak. Fresh bait and moving tides have made the difference, and calmer days let us spend more time on bigger fish. Through July 2026, blacktips cruised the beaches and Atlantic sharpnose kept the drags screaming ([July report](/fishing-reports/new-smyrna-beach-fishing-report-july-2026)).

Sharks show up in winter too. In December 2025, a crew holding their very first shark was the highlight of a snapper trip ([December report](/fishing-reports/new-smyrna-beach-fishing-report-december-2025)).

## Pair It With Something Else

Shark trips mix well with [nearshore fishing](/nearshore) for kingfish, cobia and flounder. For a calmer day, try [inshore fishing](/inshore) in the lagoon.
