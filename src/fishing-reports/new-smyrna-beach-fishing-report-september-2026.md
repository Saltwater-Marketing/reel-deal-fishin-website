Mullet were jumping into boats at Ponce Inlet this month. Snook and tarpon crowded the schools so hard the bait had nowhere else to go, and FOX 35 Orlando came out to talk with Capt. Chad about it.

### **The Mullet Run**

FOX 35's story ran on September 14, reported from the Indian River Lagoon. Capt. Chad's take: the run "makes fishing phenomenal right now."

You don't need a boat to see it, either. He pointed people to the elevated piers "up and down the coast really." [Read the full story here](/post/annual-mullet-migration-fuels-fishing-frenzy-along-central-florida-coast).

### **Redfish**

Bait stayed thick around the area, and the reds ran from smaller slot fish up to some genuinely oversized fish, in the backwaters, the creeks and the inshore waters around New Smyrna Beach and Daytona Beach.

Multiple hookups on a single trip were common, so everyone on board got a turn. Tides, weather and water conditions decided where each morning started and how we fished it.

The best part of the month was watching first time anglers and kids feel that first big run.

### **Snook**

Snook mixed in with the redfish action, including some bigger fish. A quiet morning can turn with a single strike.

### **Flounder**

After dark, our nighttime gigging trips ran through September. The flounder cooperated, with solid catches and plenty of fish headed home for the dinner table.

### **Heading Into October**

By September 18, Capt. Chad was looking ahead to fall, when the inshore bite brings chances at redfish, flounder and black drum. The Indian River Lagoon and Mosquito Lagoon kept giving us variety on top of the reds, so expect a mixed bag along with them.
