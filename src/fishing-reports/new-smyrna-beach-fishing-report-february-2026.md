We fished off the end of the New Smyrna Beach jetty for pompano in early February, and the schools kept sliding through on the current while the kids kept their rods busy.

January was inlet redfish and black drum on the flats. In February the cold water settled in, and two winter fish took over.

### **Pompano Moved Through the Jetty**

Clean water and steady current set it up at the jetty. The weather cooperated and the seas stayed manageable.

Schools slid through with the flow consistently. Once we found them, the fish were stacked tight and feeding aggressively, and limits came quick.

There wasn't much downtime between hookups. The highlight was seeing the kids get in on it, with fish hitting the deck and no long waits.

### **Cold Water Turned On the Sheepshead**

The other change was the sheepshead. When the water cools, the crustaceans move in and the sheepshead follow, and this month they were thick around docks, bridges, pilings and rock piles.

Cooler water pushed them tight to that structure. Slow presentations with natural baits did it: drop straight down, stay patient and be ready, because most bites felt like a light tap before the rod loaded up.

Capt. Chad's tip from the month: watch your line, not just the rod tip. Sheepshead are famous bait thieves, and the ones that get hooked usually eat fast and subtle.

### **For Families and First Timers**

Both bites suited families and beginners: hands on fishing, plenty of action and short waits between bites.

Sheepshead fight harder than people expect, and the firm white fillets are great on the grill or in the fryer. That makes them a good pick for anyone who wants to bring fish home.
