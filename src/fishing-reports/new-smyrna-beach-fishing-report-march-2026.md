"Had a blast with capt Chad we got are 3 man limit of sheep head." David N. left that on Google on March 24, and it's as good a summary of the month as any.

Two days before that, Erin D.'s group came within one fish: "Took us right to them. 1 away from limit." (Erin D., Google)

March was a sheepshead month around New Smyrna Beach and Port Orange. Here's what people want to know before they book one.

### **Where are the sheepshead holding?**

On the bridges, docks and other structure around New Smyrna Beach and nearby Port Orange, in the Indian River Lagoon and the backwaters. Bridge pilings and dock pilings did most of the work.

Multiple limits came over the rail this month, with plenty of solid keepers. The photos tell it: two anglers on the dock holding up sheepshead over a full spread, a crew of five at golden hour with a pelican floating behind them, and four more at sunset with their spread on the dock.

### **What are they eating?**

Live shrimp and fiddler crabs, worked tight to the pilings. The bite stayed consistent through the tide changes, so we weren't stuck waiting on one short window.

### **Are they hard to hook?**

They bite light. Feeling that tap and setting the hook at the right moment takes a little patience, and once a crew figures it out the action comes quick.

On one trip the crew stayed patient, kept their baits tight to the pilings and turned it into a great catch once the bite got dialed in.

Fishing tight to structure means some snags. Chaland L. came out with a group of five: "He also had enough rods to keep us going when lines snapped due to getting snagged up!" (Chaland L., Google)

### **Will my kids stay busy?**

That's what this bite is good at. It's hands on fishing without long waits between bites, and early in the month a family trip had the kids take over the rods and reel in fish one after another.

In one photo a girl in braids holds up her sheepshead in a towel while the angler beside her keeps fishing. In another, a boy on the bow lets out a yell with a black drum across his lap.

Families, groups of friends and first timers all got in on it. Todd B. fished with his dad: "My father and I went out with Chad and had a excellent fishing trip." (Todd B., Google) And from Sydney H.: "I was treated with great care and had so much fun! I felt safe and had a blast!" (Sydney H., Google)

### **What else is biting?**

Black drum. By the end of the month they were sharing the docks, pilings and heavier structure with the sheepshead. Drop a bait in the right spot and you feel the thump, then the rod loads up.

Jack crevalle crashed through in the second half of the month, and sharks showed up more than once, including a couple that tangled with that family crew early on.
