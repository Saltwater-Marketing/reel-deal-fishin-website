**Nearshore is the sweet spot between the shoreline and the open sea:** more pull than the flats, without the long haul of a true offshore trip. We run out of Ponce Inlet and fish up to about 6 miles off the beach, close enough to see the coast.

In early July 2026, calm seas meant doormat flounder, giant sand trout, whiting and croaker coming over the rail, with blacktip sharks cruising the beach the whole time ([July report](/fishing-reports/new-smyrna-beach-fishing-report-july-2026)). In June, a young angler landed a nice spadefish on a run offshore ([June report](/fishing-reports/new-smyrna-beach-fishing-report-june-2026)).

## A Nearshore Day

Nearshore trips leave at 7:00 AM and run 4 or 6 hours. Depending on what's moving, we cast to surface feeding schools, troll for kingfish or drop baits over nearshore structure. Flounder, sand trout, whiting and croaker are all great eating, and fish cleaning is included when regulations allow.

<h2 id="sharks">Shark Fishing</h2>

New Smyrna Beach is known as one of the most active shark fishing spots anywhere. Inlets, surf zones and nearshore structure concentrate the bait, and the sharks follow it right up to the beach.

Shark trips run 3 or 6 hours from May 1 to November 1. We put out heavy tackle and fresh bait where sharks are feeding, then wait for the drag to scream. You fight the fish. Capt. Chad coaches you from the hookset to the release and handles every shark at the boat. All sharks are catch and release.

In June 2026, several young anglers caught their first shark just off New Smyrna Beach, on a mix of blacktips, Atlantic sharpnose and a few bigger sharks ([June report](/fishing-reports/new-smyrna-beach-fishing-report-june-2026)). Sharks turn up in winter too: in December 2025, a crew's very first shark was the highlight of a snapper trip ([December report](/fishing-reports/new-smyrna-beach-fishing-report-december-2025)).

If the wind is up, an [inshore trip](/inshore) in the lagoon is the calmer call.
