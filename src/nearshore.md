**Calm seas open up the best days of the year just off New Smyrna Beach.** In early July 2026, that meant doormat flounder, giant sand trout, whiting and croaker coming over the rail, with blacktip sharks cruising the beach the whole time ([July report](/fishing-reports/new-smyrna-beach-fishing-report-july-2026)).

Nearshore is the in between: more pull than the flats, without the long haul of a true offshore trip. Most days we fish just a few miles off the beach, close enough to see the shoreline.

## What a Nearshore Day Looks Like

We launch from New Smyrna Beach and run straight out the inlet. Depending on what's hot, we cast to surface feeding schools, troll for kingfish or drop baits over the nearshore reefs and structure.

The fish change with the season. Kingfish provide drag scorching runs from spring through fall, tarpon move through in summer, and cobia show up shadowing rays. On the nearshore reefs, bonito crash baits with blistering runs. In June 2026, a young angler even landed a nice spadefish on a run offshore ([June report](/fishing-reports/new-smyrna-beach-fishing-report-june-2026)).

## Fish for the Table

Nearshore days are one of the best ways to bring home dinner. Flounder, sand trout, whiting and croaker are all great eating, and fish cleaning is included when regulations allow.

## Sharks Are Part of the Deal

Blacktips, bonnetheads and Atlantic sharpnose cruise the beaches through the warm months. If your crew wants a trip built around them, book a [shark fishing charter](/shark-fishing). If the wind is up, an [inshore trip](/inshore) in the lagoon is the calmer call.
