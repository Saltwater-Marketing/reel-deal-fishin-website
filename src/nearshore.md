**Nearshore is the sweet spot between the shoreline and the open sea:** more pull than the flats, without the long haul of a true offshore trip. We run out of Ponce Inlet and fish up to about 6 miles off the beach, close enough to see the coast.

When the seas lay down in summer, that can mean doormat flounder, giant sand trout, whiting and croaker coming over the rail, with blacktip sharks cruising the beach the whole time ([July report](/fishing-reports/new-smyrna-beach-fishing-report-july-2026)). One June trip, a young angler landed a nice spadefish ([June report](/fishing-reports/new-smyrna-beach-fishing-report-june-2026)).

## A Nearshore Day Off New Smyrna Beach

Nearshore trips leave New Smyrna Beach at 7:00 AM and run 4 or 6 hours. Depending on what's moving, we cast to surface feeding schools, troll for kingfish or drop baits over nearshore structure. Flounder, sand trout, whiting and croaker are all great eating, and fish cleaning is included when regulations allow.

## Sharks Are Part of the Deal

Blacktips and Atlantic sharpnose cruise the New Smyrna Beach shoreline through the warm months, so sharks often turn up on nearshore days. If your crew wants a trip built around them, book a [shark fishing charter](/shark-fishing).

If the wind is up, an [inshore trip](/inshore) in the lagoon is the calmer call. Staying in Daytona? Nearshore days fit a [Daytona Beach](/daytona-beach) visit too, since we launch a short drive south.
